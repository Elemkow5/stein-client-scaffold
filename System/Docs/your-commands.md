# Your Commands

Everything you can type into Claude Code to work with your system. You don't need to memorize these — but knowing they exist helps.

---

## Daily Use

### `/daily`
Your morning ritual. Run this when you open your laptop.

Pulls today's calendar, your open tasks, your priorities, and anything from your inbox. Lets you plan your day interactively — update tasks, flag things, set your focus. Different from the morning email brief: `/daily` is a live conversation, the email is a passive summary.

**When to use it:** Every morning, or any time you want a real-time read on your day.

---

### `/capture`
Fast brain dump. Anything you need to remember but can't deal with right now.

Takes whatever you throw at it — a name, an idea, a task, a link, a voice note — and drops it in your inbox for later. No friction. No thinking about where it goes.

**When to use it:** Anytime you want to park something without losing it.

---

### `/inbox`
Works through your capture queue.

Reads everything in your inbox and routes each item to the right place: People file, task board, session log, or wherever it belongs. You confirm or redirect as it goes.

**When to use it:** When your inbox has piled up — usually every few days.

---

### `/recall`
Searches everything.

Natural language — ask it anything. "What did we decide about the proposal?" "When did I last talk to [name]?" "What's the status of [project]?" It searches session logs, People files, your task board, and your library.

**When to use it:** Anytime you need to find something you know is in the system somewhere.

---

### `/wrap`
End-of-session log. Run this when you're done with a meaningful block of work.

Logs what happened, syncs any new tasks, updates open items, and closes the session cleanly. Keeps your session history complete so future `/recall` searches actually find things.

**When to use it:** After any significant work session — a call, a project push, a decision-making session.

---

## Weekly / Less Frequent

### `/checkin`
Weekly pulse check.

Reviews open projects, checks priority alignment, surfaces things that have gone quiet or are at risk. Good for stepping back from daily execution and asking whether you're working on the right things.

**When to use it:** Once a week, or any time you feel like you've lost the thread.

---

### `/goal`
Work with a specific goal or priority.

Lets you define, update, or review a strategic goal. Connects to your Priorities file and helps you think through what's actually moving the needle.

**When to use it:** When setting or revisiting a priority, or when something feels misaligned.

---

### `/plan`
Build an execution plan for a task or project.

Takes a task and produces a structured step-by-step plan before any work starts. Useful for anything non-trivial that spans multiple sessions or has dependencies.

**When to use it:** Before starting something complex — not for simple one-off tasks.

---

### `/debrief`
Post-event or post-project reflection.

Walks through what happened, what worked, what didn't, and what to carry forward. Useful after a pitch, a launch, a big client call, or anything worth learning from.

**When to use it:** After any significant event you want to extract lessons from.

---

### `/log`
Manual session log entry.

Adds a timestamped entry to your session log. Use when you want to record something mid-session without doing a full `/wrap`.

**When to use it:** When something worth remembering happens and you don't want to wait until `/wrap`.

---

## Situational

### `/comms-triage`
Works through a pile of messages — email, Slack, or both.

Reads your unread messages, prioritizes them, and helps you decide what to reply to, what to defer, and what to ignore. Writes draft replies if you want them.

**When to use it:** When you're behind on messages and need to clear the queue efficiently.

---

### `/comms-reply`
Drafts a reply to a specific message.

Give it the thread or paste the message, tell it what you want to say, and it writes the reply in your voice. You edit and send.

**When to use it:** For any reply that needs more than a few words — especially anything nuanced or high-stakes.

---

### `/commitment`
Add or review an open commitment.

Logs something you've promised — to someone or to yourself — with a due date and who it's owed to. Keeps your commitments ledger clean and current.

**When to use it:** After any call or email where you committed to doing something.

---

### `/brief`
Pulls context on a person, project, or topic before a meeting.

Tell it who you're meeting or what you're walking into, and it assembles relevant notes from your People files, session logs, and project history. Gives you a quick brief so you're not going in cold.

**When to use it:** 15-30 minutes before any important call or meeting.

---

### `/handoff` and `/continue`
Pause and resume across sessions.

`/handoff` saves exactly where you are — what you were working on, what's in progress, what's next. `/continue` picks up from that save point in a new session. Useful when you're mid-project and need to stop cleanly.

**When to use it:** When you need to stop mid-task and want to resume exactly where you left off.

---

### `/update`
Update your system with new information.

Tell it what's changed — a project status, a person's role, a priority shift — and it routes the update to the right files. Faster than editing files manually when you just want to record a change.

**When to use it:** When something important changed and you want the system to reflect it.

---

### `/recall`
*(Also listed under Daily Use — worth repeating.)*

The most-used command after `/daily`. If you can't find something, try `/recall` before anything else.

---

## If Something's Wrong

### `/stein-doctor`
Diagnostic check.

Scans your system for common issues — missing files, broken integrations, stale configuration. Tells you what's off and what to do about it.

**When to use it:** If something feels wrong, if a command isn't behaving as expected, or after a major change to your setup.

---

*Don't see a command you need? Ask Claude directly — it can do a lot that isn't wrapped in a named command.*
