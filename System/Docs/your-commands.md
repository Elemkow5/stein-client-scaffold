# Your Commands

Everything you can type into Claude Code to work with your system.

There are ten. You don't need to memorize them — you'll use three of them constantly and the rest when the moment calls for it. If you only ever remember one, make it `/checkin`.

---

## Every Day

### `/checkin`
Your one ritual. Run this when you open your laptop.

It asks whether you want a **daily**, **weekly**, or **quarterly** check-in, then runs the right one. That's the whole point — one command to remember instead of three.

- **Daily** — today's calendar, your open tasks, your priorities, and what actually matters today. Interactive: update tasks, flag things, set your focus.
- **Weekly** — reviews open projects, checks priority alignment, surfaces what's gone quiet or is at risk. This is also where any commitments the system flagged during the week come to you for confirmation.
- **Quarterly** — full review against your goals, and setting the next quarter's.

Different from the morning email brief: `/checkin` is a live conversation, the email is a passive summary that arrives whether you show up or not.

**When to use it:** Daily every morning. Weekly once a week — Friday or Sunday. Quarterly when a quarter turns.

---

### `/capture`
Fast brain dump. Anything you need to remember but can't deal with right now.

Takes whatever you throw at it — a name, an idea, a task, a link — and drops it in your inbox for later. No friction. No thinking about where it goes.

**When to use it:** Anytime you want to park something without losing it. Especially right after a call, for anything you promised that the system can't see.

---

### `/recall`
Searches everything.

Natural language — ask it anything. "What did we decide about the proposal?" "When did I last talk to [name]?" "What's the status of [project]?" It searches your session history, People files, task board, goals, decisions, and knowledge.

This is the payoff for everything the system does in the background. If you can't find something, try this before anything else.

**When to use it:** Anytime you need something you know is in the system somewhere.

## Clearing the Decks

### `/inbox`
Works through your capture queue.

Reads everything in your inbox and routes each item to the right place: People file, task board, session log, or wherever it belongs. You confirm or redirect as it goes.

**When to use it:** When your inbox has piled up — usually every few days.

---

### `/wrap`
End-of-session close. Run this when you're done with a meaningful block of work.

Logs what happened, captures next steps onto your task board, checks open commitments, and closes the session cleanly. Keeps your history complete so future `/recall` searches actually find things.

**When to use it:** After any significant work session — a call, a project push, a decision-making session.

---

## Around Meetings

### `/brief`
Pulls context on a person, project, or topic before a meeting.

Tell it who you're meeting or what you're walking into, and it assembles everything the system knows: last conversation, open items, what you owe them, what they owe you. If you haven't spoken in over a month, it flags that too.

**When to use it:** 15–30 minutes before any important call.

---

### `/debrief`
The other half of `/brief`. Run it right after.

Paste your notes or a transcript — or just talk it through — and it extracts what was decided, what you committed to, and what needs following up. Updates the person's file and logs the interaction.

**When to use it:** Immediately after any meeting worth remembering. This is the habit that makes `/brief` good six months from now.

---

### `/commitment`
Logs a promise — what, to whom, by when.

Writes to your commitments ledger, your task board, and that person's file, so it surfaces automatically the next time you're about to see them.

The system flags commitment language it spots in your email and Slack, but it never adds anything to your ledger without you confirming it. Anything you promised out loud on a call is invisible to it — that's what this command is for.

**When to use it:** After any call or email where you said you'd do something.

---

## Thinking Ahead

### `/goal`
Work with a specific goal or priority.

A real goal-setting conversation — what you're moving toward and what you're moving away from, who it's for, what's at stake, how confident you actually are, then milestones and the tasks under them. Syncs deadlines to your calendar and task board.

**When to use it:** When setting a new goal, or when something feels misaligned and you need to think it through properly.

---

### `/plan`
Build an execution plan for a task or project.

Takes something complicated and produces a structured plan before any work starts — approach, steps, what "done" looks like. The plan survives across sessions, so you don't lose the thread halfway through.

**When to use it:** Before starting anything that spans more than one sitting. Not for simple tasks.

## A Few Notes

**These ten are your starting set.** There are more commands available — deeper session tools, unified inbox triage across email and Slack, system diagnostics. They're not here because a system you can learn in one sitting is a system you'll actually use. When you want more, ask and we'll turn them on.

**You're not limited to commands.** Just talk to Claude normally. It has your full context loaded — your projects, your people, your priorities, your history. The commands are shortcuts for things you'll do repeatedly, not a menu you're confined to.

**If something feels broken,** tell me. Don't spend your time debugging it.
